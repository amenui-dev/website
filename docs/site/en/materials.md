# Materials and looseness

A material is how the second pass draws: plain, dot, halftone, ascii, print, lino, glitch, near, scribblr. At rest they look like plain (but for the few with a look of their own); loosened, each has its own. `El::material(id)` gives a subtree another material. Scrolling fast, each material leaves its own trace (DESIGN §1.8.3).

`scribblr` draws no differently at rest: its second pass is plain's frame. Loosened, an element's fill fades and its ink becomes one thread of constant width (`src/strand.rs`), drawn as shapes in the first pass: along the glyphs' true outlines, then joined into one stroke by straight bridges, then wandering, knotting where the field map has energy and trailing off along its flow; drawn back, the knots go first and the fill comes back. Text to read only gets a line on its outline. `Look::hold_ms` holds the line's pose for a beat (83 ms is twelve frames a second). `El::trace(Trace)` draws a long path by progress under any material, solid or dashed, pushed by the map's flow, its head stirring the map.
