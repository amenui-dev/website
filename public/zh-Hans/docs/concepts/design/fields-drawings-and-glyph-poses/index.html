1.26 场、绘图和字形姿态

03.42

本页只有英文。

The experiments of fields, the Lab and the decode transition were first broken down into primitives from first principles, to see what the foundation lacked:

ExperimentComes down toWhat was missing
Lit text (Lumen)Each glyph's tone by its distance to the lightNo access to each laid-out glyph
ContoursScalar field → grid sampling → marching squares → polylines → strokesNo fields, and nothing could draw a slanted line (2D instances couldn't turn)
SilkMany points advected by a vector field (curl of noise + flow stirred by the pointer)motes::FlowField was special-purpose code with field and drawing welded together
GrownReaction–diffusion masked by an element's true coverageThe state couldn't get an element's true outline
MercuryMetaball scalar field → contours + dots by valueAs for contours
Text on a path / sphere (Flow, Loupe)Each glyph's translation, rotation, scale and opacityInstances had no transforms of their own
Orbits, spring, card stackPoints and strokes; springsOnly element boxes could be drawn

So the foundation changed in five places, with no special cases in the effect pages:

  • Per-instance transforms (Instance::xf, the eighth vec4): a 2×2 linear transform about the instance's rect center, stored as "minus the identity", so zero means no transform and no existing constructor changed. The vertex shader turns the four corners by it, and exact_i maps pixels back into local box coordinates, so atlas coverage and distance and shapes' analytic distance are computed on the turned outline and materials work as usual. Instance::bounds gives the bounding box after turning, used by partial redraw. Sampling follows the transform too: morph::cover_at first maps the point back into the box through xf (morph::to_box, like the shader's exact_i), and cells and ink_bounds use the turned bounding box, so a posed or turned glyph loosens into particles from where it is drawn, and on_outline masks and decode blocks are there as well (a_posed_glyph_loosens_from_where_it_is_drawn in tests/transform.rs).
  • Glyph poses (El::pose(|GlyphAt| Pose)): asked once per laid-out glyph when the scene is built (index, count, center, content box, the range of all glyph centers, and the pen as laid out: pen, the x of its origin before the glyph's image is put on a quarter pixel, base, its baseline as drawn, and advance, kerning in it; a pose that moves a glyph by target − pen puts its pen at target), giving a translation, an angle, two scales, multipliers for tone and opacity, and a minimum accent; glyphs with opacity 0 aren't drawn. No layout or shaping: pointer movement only rebuilds the scene. Posed text is culled by where its glyphs go, not by its box: a box outside its clip still draws the glyphs posed into it, and the glyphs posed outside the clip are not made.
  • Drawings (el::canvas(Drawing), src/draw.rs): an element whose content is marks in its own coordinate space (Mark::Dot, Mark::Line), scaled to the element's width with the height in proportion. Each mark is a shape instance: a dot is a rounded square and a stroke a turned bar with round ends, so they have true outlines, every material can draw them and fronts can loosen them. Marks change every frame, so they don't condense (their birth time is long past). Pointer handlers on a canvas (on_move, on_drag, and El::on_point, which combines move, press-drag and leave in one handler, fingers included) report coordinates in the canvas's space.
  • A stroke with a ramp (Drawing::ramp(a, b, w, paint, mid, far), Mark::Line's far and mid): its colour and opacity go from paint at a to far at b, through mid (a paint and where it is, 0–1 of the way) if given, still one instance (the far and middle paints packed into its uv.zw, their hues and the middle's place into extra.x, all negative; a line without a ramp is the instance it was). The instance's opacity is its strongest stop's and each stop's is a share of it (the start's in extra.y), so whatever scales an element's opacity later (a fade, a flow's wait, a see-through group) scales every stop: a ramp waiting at opacity 0 draws nothing. The ramp's parameter is the pixel's projection on a→b, clamp(dot(p − a, b − a) / |b − a|², 0, 1), so each round end is its end's colour, flat. Over a picture (where ink is written as its own colour) the stops' colours are mixed in OKLab, each piece linear with no smoothing at the middle stop, and the opacity, linear along the length, is applied after the mix. Off a picture ink is a theme mix with one accent hue a pixel, so there the paint's numbers (tone, accent, opacity) go in a line and it keeps paint's hue all the way: a ramp within one hue, or to transparent, comes out as drawn there; one from one hue to another is reported (ViewProblem::RampHue, a P1 in the audit). 540 ramps are one draw.
  • A drawing's own colours (Drawing::palette([...]), sRGB 0–1): a mark's Paint::hue(3 + k) is the drawing's colour k in place of a theme accent, its tone and accent mixing towards it as towards one, so a piece's marks are the same wherever they are drawn (its page, a window onto it, a band) whatever the theme there. The runtime gathers the drawings' colours each frame into one table of at most 32 (draw::OWN_COLOURS; the same colour in two drawings counts once, in the order first drawn), renumbers each mark's hue into it (3 + its place) and hands it to the shader with the theme's (Scene::own; a colour that changes under the same hue redraws everything). Over a picture, where ink is written as its own colour, they are exact and a ramp between two is mixed in OKLab as between theme hues (a ramp packs its stops' hues in 6 bits each). Off a picture, where ink is one of the theme's three accents a pixel, a mark in one of them is drawn in hue 0 and reported (ViewProblem::OwnColourOffPicture, a P1 in the audit; what is drawn of a mark, cut by its clip, is what must be on a picture), as a ramp across hues is there; so is a hue past the drawing's palette or the table, and outside a drawing a hue of 3 or more is hue 0. tests/own_colours.rs (W-04's ray in its own three colours over its sky, by hand in OKLab within 2 / 255 under a dark and a light theme and the same pixels under both; off a picture hue 0 and a P1; two drawings sharing a colour count it once).
  • Blades (Mark::Blade, Drawing::blade): a rooted stroke that the field map (§1.29) bends, for a field of grass. root, len, width (at the root, 1.5 by default; the tip is half of it, 0.5 px at least), its own lean (0), max_lean (57°), stiff (0.5), paint, lean_paint and an optional head glyph. Its lean is lean + max_lean · clamp(flow.x / drift, −1, 1) with the flow read at the root, so vertical flow never lays it down: its own lean and the flow's add, all of it within ±max_lean (and ±85°); its colour is mix(paint, lean_paint, m) and its head's mix(head.paint, lean_paint, m), where m (Blade::mix) is |lean| / max_lean by default (lean_from 0, lean_full 0), and with lean_full above lean_from 0 up to lean_from, 1 from lean_full and a smoothstep between (a field that greens only once it really leans: 8° and 20° on the website). The lean is the angle of the tip's tangent from upright. Along the blade, a share s of its length from the root, the tangent is lean · (0.35 + 0.65 · g(s)) (Blade::tangent): the root leans 0.35 of the tip. With stiff 0, g(s) = s, one even bend from root to tip; with stiff above 0, g is 0 over the lower 0.6 · stiff of the blade (that part is straight) and then a smoothstep to 1 at the tip. What falls over is the head, not the whole stalk laid flat: the chord's angle is about two thirds of the lean with stiff 0 (38° at 57°, 49° at 72°) and less when it is stiffer (33° and 41° at 0.5). The shape (Blade::at, Blade::shape, the same in the shader) is walked by arc length in 16 steps, so the length is kept. It is drawn as 10 segments when it is longer than 48 logical px, 6 when longer than 24 and 2 otherwise, their ends spaced evenly in (s + 3 · g(s)) / 4 so the segments are shorter where it bends and no two neighbours are more than 12° apart: strokes with round ends cut along the bisector where two meet, tapering, the first and last pulled in by half their width so the blade runs exactly from root to tip. The head's ink box has its bottom middle on the tip and its up along the tip's tangent; the stem ends there. All of it is worked out in the vertex stage from the map at the root (blade_seg, blade_head): while only the map moves, nothing is laid out, painted or built on the CPU, and the instances stay as they are. A blade is never pushed by fronts or the flow (it leans instead) and redraws wherever the map changes under its reach. Inside a turned element (El::turn) a blade is not turned with it.
  • Fields (src/field.rs): Scalar (any Fn(x, y) -> f32), Noise (value noise with time), Bump, Balls (metaballs) and Sum; Grid (sampled at nodes, read back bilinearly, contours producing polylines with marching squares, saddles decided by the cell average so lines never cross); curl (divergence-free flow); Flow (a vector grid; stir adds velocity, fade returns it to rest); Swarm (points advected by a velocity field); GrayScott (masked reaction–diffusion: u = 1, v = 0 outside the mask, no flux across the outline). The hash and noise of motes were replaced by these, and the old copies deleted. Drawing::dots and Drawing::contours are two ways to draw a field.
  • Two things an element tells the state: El::on_visible(|bool| ..) (entering or leaving view: its box intersects its clip; experiments out of view don't step) and El::on_outline(cols, rows, |Grid| ..) (the subtree's true outline, hidden instances included, as coverage on a grid by morph::cover_at; sent only when the instances changed). Both are messages the runtime queues after layout or scene building and hands to update together (Ui::flush); the state never changes in the middle of drawing.

Acceptance: unit tests in field.rs (a circle's contour is closed and on the radius, open at the edges, noise is continuous and deterministic, curl has no divergence, reaction–diffusion grows only inside the mask and deterministically, a stirred flow comes to rest); tests/lab.rs (each experiment runs deterministically in the headless runtime, Grown receives its outline and grows only inside it, lit text has light and dark).

Depth and facing: text on a form. A pose also has a depth and a facing: Pose::z (logical px, positive toward the viewer) and Pose::facing (1 facing the viewer, 0 edge on, negative facing away). The defaults, 0 and 1, draw exactly as before. The glyphs of one text element are drawn far to near by z (a stable sort; there is no depth buffer). El::form(FormFx) keeps such text legible, with four numbers:

namewhatrangestepdefault
backopacity kept by glyphs facing away0–10.050
edgeglyphs facing less than this fade out; at 60 % of it they are not drawn0–0.60.050.25
overlapa glyph whose ink is covered by nearer glyphs more than this is not drawn0.1–10.050.35
fogtone of the farthest glyphs (the nearest keep theirs)0–10.050.6

overlap is how dense a form may be, and it is measured on the true ink, not guessed from spacing: going from near to far, the turned bounding boxes find the candidates, then morph::cover_at on a 7 × 7 grid over the glyph's ink measures how much of it the nearer drawn glyphs cover; the samples come out of the frame budget (budget::grant), and past it the boxes alone decide.

El::reveal_by(Reveal::Word | Line | Glyph) says how clips, poses and the window take text away. With Word (or Line), a word (a line) is drawn whole or not at all: it takes the faintest of its glyphs' opacities, it is left out when any of its ink would be cut by its clip or the window, and on a form it is thinned as one (left out if any of its glyphs is covered past overlap). Words are Unicode word boundaries with the punctuation after a word belonging to it (Text::reveal_groups). Glyph is the default and is what happened before.

The amenui::form module wraps a block of text onto a shape seen by a mesh::Camera. Form { shape, spin, camera, cover }: the shape is Shape::Sphere(r), Shape::Surface(f) (how far the surface is along each direction) or Shape::Mesh; spin turns it (yaw and pitch, degrees); cover is how much of it the block covers (a fraction of the longitudes and of the latitudes). Form::pose_of(GlyphAt) (or form.pose() for El::pose) gives each glyph its place, turn and scale on the surface, with z and facing filled in; across and down use the same angular step, so glyphs keep their proportions where the shape faces you. Form::hit(x, y, w, h) casts the pointer's ray and returns FormHit { point, normal, t, uv }, the place on the surface in the block's own terms (analytic for a sphere, Mesh::ray for a mesh, a march and bisection for a surface function), so what the pointer lights follows the form as it turns, and a pointer off the form finds nothing.

Hits follow what is drawn. Ui::glyph_at(x, y) -> Option<GlyphHit { key, i, z, local }> answers with the nearest glyph drawn at that point, after poses, turns and thinning (hit::glyph_under over the last paint's glyphs, Ui::glyphs); El::on_point gets the same in Point::glyph and Point::z while hovering (during a drag they are None and 0). An element turned with El::turn(radians) is drawn turned about its centre, and so is its subtree; hits go through the same transform (hit::Affine, hit::contains), so a turned button is pressed where it is drawn, not in the empty corners of its box. A 3D hit says where it is: MeshHit has point (world), normal, t (along the ray) and uv (the place in the view); Pick is unchanged.

A press goes to the nearest element under the pointer that takes a press or a drag: a button or link inside an element that drags (a scene hearing the pointer with on_point) gets its own press, and the outer element's drag starts on its own area or on something inside it that takes no press. Fingers follow the same rule.

The recipe form3d (docs/recipes/form3d.md) is a passage on a sphere with edge 0.5, words revealed whole, the words around the pointer's place on the surface lit, and a drag that turns it.

Acceptance of depth and forms: tests/form.rs (a passage on a sphere turned a whole way round in twelve steps draws no glyph facing away, no two drawn glyphs cover more of each other's ink than overlap (+0.15 for the grid), depths never fall along the drawing order, the same turn gives the same instances, words come and go whole, a pose with the default depth and facing draws exactly as before, Form::hit finds the glyph drawn under the pointer); tests/pointer.rs (after a quarter turn of the sphere the glyph glyph_at gives at the same point is the one now drawn there; a square turned 30° presses at its drawn corner and not at its box's empty corner; a button inside a pointer scene takes its own press, and the scene still drags from its own area and from text inside it); unit tests in mesh.rs (a ray hits the unit sphere at length 1 with the normal along the point, within 1e-4) and form.rs.
