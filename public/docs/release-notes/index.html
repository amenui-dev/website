Versions follow Semantic Versioning ↗ as Cargo reads it: before 1.0, a change to the second number (0.1 → 0.2) may break code, and a change to the third (0.1.0 → 0.1.1) does not. The crate and the amenui npm package (web/) always carry the same version, because the page side must match the module that web_app! generates. The minimum Rust is the one CI builds with (rust-version in Cargo.toml); raising it is a breaking change before 1.0 only in the second number.

Marks only while the cards move

  • A subtree marked El::audit_under(preset) is audited under that preset's rules, its accent share of its own box, and the rest of the page under the page's (render --preset) or the floors alone. Breaking: audit::audit takes the theme, audit::audit_under takes the preset (not its name and rules), Props and Node have audit_under, and Driven::audit and Driven::audit_under have no default body: a wrapper that forwarded only audit (so a preset run silently used the base audit) now has to forward audit_under too.
  • A preset carries rules (Preset::rules, preset::Rule: contrast for running text or all text to read, text size, margin, measure, accent share) that the audit holds its pages to as well (audit::audit_under, render --preset, drive::in_preset); the ten presets each have theirs. A rule can only tighten: a preset with a contrast under 4.5 or a text size under 9 px is refused when read (PresetError::Looser). Breaking: audit::Kind has Margin, Measure and Accent; LookError has UnknownPreset and BadPreset, PresetError has Looser; Preset has rules (older JSON reads with none).
  • Under Glitch, text to read is kept clear of the chroma offset and most of the scanlines (PostSpec::clear_text, off by default): on its ink and 2 px round it the offset is 0 and the scanlines 0.08. Pictures, shapes, the ground and legible(false) text keep the whole pass. The audit reads a frame bent by curvature where the bend drew it (audit::unbend; with no curvature, the frame as drawn), and reports text the bend carries past the window's edge as Clipped (audit::bent_off). Breaking: PostSpec and Post have clear_text, Scene has legible.
  • The audit reports a cut as Clipped only where no scroll view can bring that edge's side into view: not the last row of a page that scrolls, cut by the window's bottom edge (the fold), nor a line in a strip that scrolls sideways cut by the strip's end or the window's. A box in the scroll view that does not scroll still clips what it cuts, and a scroll view clips across the way it scrolls.
  • The audit tells large text by its size (audit::LARGE_PX 24, LARGE_BOLD_PX 19 when bold): it needs 3:1, a P0 under it, and a preset's contrast rules do not apply to it. A 64 px headline at 3.28:1 passes.
  • At rest a card stack's flat cards are on whole device pixels however large the box is drawn (a page's El::fit zoom too): each side's outline is one pixel at its grey, where the top and right ones had been two at half strength. Mesh3d.snap names a rectangle an orthographic view puts on whole device pixels (Scene3d::proj_in); the card stack's scene sets it to its top card. Breaking: Mesh3d has snap. A 3D element's edge on a pixel boundary reads as exactly there (the resolve's zero on a whole step, and distances within a step of a half pixel taken as that), so it covers the pixel inside fully and the one outside not at all.
  • The pointer leaving an element whose messages are its own (El::local_messages) restages it as a move over it does; the leave had viewed and laid out the whole page. On the Lab's loupe this takes a 10 ms frame each time the pointer leaves the glass down to an ordinary one.
  • Under dottr and charr a thin line at looseness 1 is a row of the least marks instead of falling between the cells: a loose cell with ink in it sampled below 0.25 (INK_FLOOR) is drawn as if sampled at 0.25 (dottr a dot, charr the lightest character, centred on the line). Cells sampled at 0.25 or more are drawn as before; halftonr is unchanged.
  • A card stack under dottr, charr and halftonr is drawn as under plain at rest: its cards loosen to 1 within 120 ms while a card's spring moves (LOOSEN_S) and back to 0 within 240 ms after the last one stops (TIGHTEN_S), and then ask for no frames. They had been held at looseness 1. Stack::moving is true until they are tight.

Faster scrolling, no late stalls

  • The reduced grid (stages at their own scale, a quality below 1) is fixed to the content of the largest scroll area, and the filter up weighs its texels itself: a stage a scroll carries is no longer shaded again in every moved frame. The reduced target is three texels larger each way.
  • While the trace holds the rows, the frame moves as the viewport's ground does: an opaque shape with a material override under the whole viewport (a plain page over a dotted look, as the components pages are) is that ground. Their plain content had been shaded again over the whole viewport every frame as another lattice.
  • While the trace holds the rows, a scroll area inside the moving one that does not scroll itself no longer makes every frame be drawn whole ("latch: not alone"): it is content the frame carries, shaded again where it was and where it is. On /components/ that was 81 frames in 180 of a fast back-and-forth scroll.
  • With coalesced input (the web and desktop hosts), what a scroll brings into view no longer condenses as if it were new: the paint left for the frame is a scroll's when only scrolls asked for it, and when a scroll and something else (rows added, a new page) asked for it, what was in the layout before does not condense and new keys do. A fast scroll had condensed most of the screen every frame.
  • A wheel gesture stays with what took its first step (WHEEL_LATCH = 0.5 s between steps, the pointer still): a 3D view (El::on_wheel, widgets::orbit) scrolled under the pointer no longer takes over a page's scroll momentum, which stopped the page and laid it out again every frame. An on_wheel handler that returns None leaves the step to scrolling. A layout a handler's message asks for is put down to the handler's call in FramePath::layout_from (was Ui::send).
  • The runtime hashes its own maps and fingerprints with fasthash::FastHasher (a folded multiply), not SipHash.
  • A scroll step's paint allocates about a third less and half the bytes: text's glyph lists are kept between paints, layout-cache look-ups no longer copy the text, the field map is not built to be compared each tick, and recording texts index the scene once.
  • While a scroll goes on, Ui::a11y_diff sends only focus: the mirror's new places go once the scroll rests (Ui::next_wake asks for that frame). Every scroll frame on the web had rebuilt the mirror and sent every element in the scroll area again, serialised.
  • FramePath::layouts and layout_from: the layouts since the frame before and where the first was asked for (the Ui::relayout call's file and line), shown at the end of FramePath::line as layout 2 (src/ui.rs:2081). Ui::layouts_since_drawn.
  • A partial frame draws stage 1's boxes in one render pass for each of its two images (was a pair of passes for each box: the M1 Lab's median frame opened 8, each loading and storing both images whole on a tile-based GPU). Cost::passes counts them.
  • FramePath::shade_why: the three reasons whose boxes cover most of a partial frame's shaded pixels, each as a share of the window, shown after the share in FramePath::line (shade 54% (stage moved 43%, changed 12%, band 6%)).
  • A 3D element supersampled at a whole scale (the card stack, at 2) reads each pixel's texel from the pixel's own position, exactly: one driver lost the outline's top row and another its bottom.
  • A cached subtree (El::cached) in a scroll area moves with the scroll: the frame moves its plate as it moves the last frame and inks only the band scrolled into view and what changed in it (every scroll frame of the Lab's Loupe was drawn whole, "plate emptied"). Any change to a cached subtree now inks its plate only within the frame's boxes. PlateSpan has a new field, replace.
  • An instance whose clip alone changed (a clipping box's edge crossing a viewport's) is drawn again between the two edges, not in its whole box: the frame a cached sheet's edge scrolls past drew most of the window.
  • A frame that moves the last one redraws, for what came into view or went, each instance's box within its clip (was one box round each run of them, which on the Lab spanned the window's width): stage 1 shades about half the pixels it did on a fast scroll.
  • A scroll's trace pass probes the nearness as far apart as it reads (was every 4 px): the same frame, with fewer reads for each pixel it tests (it shades a little more of the viewport again, which costs less than the reads saved: on the Lab a fast-scroll frame took about a quarter less on the CPU rasteriser).
  • The warm-up makes the trace pass (it was compiled on the first fast scroll, a stall of seconds), and after the warm-up, idle time makes every material pass, upsample and trace pass the page may need (Renderer::preparing; was one a drawn frame, so a page at rest made none before its first scroll).
  • Each material pipeline (the finishing pass, the upsample, the trace pass, a material's own passes) is drawn with once, into one pixel, as it is made: the first frame that needs it no longer waits for a driver to finish it (the first switch to a dark scheme with bloom took twice as long as the later ones on the CPU rasteriser).
  • Rows of another lattice (or none) in a viewport no longer draw the frame whole while the trace holds the rows: the frame moves as the page's material does, and what is in the other material is shaded again.
  • A plain shape behind a whole scroll area (a page's fill(Paint::GROUND)) moves with the scroll as the ground does: a slow scroll of the components pages shaded about 85 % of the window each frame, now the band and the edges.

A Claude Code skill and a save hook

  • .claude/skills/amenui/SKILL.md is AGENTS.md as the amenui skill (made by tools/skill.sh; tests/skill.rs fails when they differ).
  • .claude/settings.json runs tools/audit-hook.sh after Edit and Write: a change to a recipe, a component's examples or its widget renders that one page with render --audit --strict and hands its P0 and P1 findings and the view's problems back to the model.
  • render --page component-<name>: a component's examples, one under another, as a page to render and audit.

Examples in English; near at rest without the accent

  • The component examples and the widgets' Usage: lines show English only (Badge "New" and "Draft", Avatar "Grace Hopper", ...); Badge's "New" is Primary, not Accent, and Button's disabled example is a disabled Primary.
  • near draws its bands inside the ink in the hairline grey (was the accent): at rest a material shows no accent.

A text's pens to the state, and its glyphs as drawn

  • El::on_pens(f): a text sends its glyphs' pens as the shaper set them (logical px in the content box, the numbers GlyphAt::pen gives a pose) after a paint, when they changed; a state no longer collects them from inside a pose.
  • Ui::glyphs_drawn(key) -> Vec<GlyphDrawn>: each glyph of a text as the last paint drew it, after its pose: its number, the box its raster covers (logical px, window coordinates) and its opacity.
  • A message the runtime queues in a paint (on_outline, on_pens) asks for the next frame.

near in the ink only; knowing a cached page has no kept image

  • near at rest is the ink and its greys only: outside ink its three nearest bands are the ink at 0.5, 0.3 and 0.15 (was the second accent; none further out), and where loose the dim grey at 0.35 (was the third, the warning colour). Inside ink the bands stay hairline.
  • Ui::on_kept(f) and Ui::kept_image(key): a cached element over the plates' limit (four, twice the window's area) has no kept image and a cached_view of it draws nothing; f is told its key and false, then true when it has one again or goes, and the page is viewed again on that frame.

A card stack's turned outlines are smooth

  • Mesh3d::resolution_scale goes up to 2 (was 1): above 1 the mesh pass is supersampled, so a silhouette and its drawn outline fall on a pixel's share of them. The card stack draws at 2: a turned card's hairline is a smooth line, its whole pixels still exactly the grey asked (was steps of two pixels, at 0.5).

A held stage costs nothing between its draws

  • A slow stage holding its image no longer paints the page on every frame it holds: a frame in which it would hold paints nothing for it, a step that comes while it would hold waits for its next draw (not viewed), and it draws alone, the rest of the page copied, on its own cadence and on the first frame without input. A stage that held draws whole then, not by its parts (El::steps inside it), so the page comes to rest after it.

A card stack's outlines, card by card

  • Stack::outlines: card n's outline grey (the last entry repeats; empty: the ink). The default stack is raised cards (paints [RAISED], was the three accents, ink and dim), outlined 1 px, the top one in the ink and the ones under it in the hairline grey.
  • MeshInstances::outline (and MeshInstances::outlined): the grey a group of copies' drawn outline (Mesh3d::outline) takes, by its tone; None is the ink as before. **A struct literal of MeshInstances needs outline: None** (MeshInstances::one sets it).

The accent only under the hand, in charts and examples

  • dot_chart: the picked range is ink (its dots up to the ink, the line under the axis ink) and the rest of the field dim; the accent is only on the dot under the pointer (DotChart::under) and on a control point and its tick while dragged (DotChart::dragged).
  • avatar's last argument is a shade, not a hue: 0 ink, 1 dim, 2 text (widgets::SHADES), initials in the ground.
  • The examples: a main action is Variant::Primary (counter, morph, mesh, chapter), and nothing is in the accent that is not held or dragged or a named specimen (counter's shape, morph's disc, chapter's numbers and annotate's panel are ink; annotate's meter and loading's pictures are greys, not hues; the caption specimen is raised in a hairline frame). badge's usage line shows Variant::Primary.

Menus and sheets are raised

  • widgets::MENU_SURFACE is the raised grey (it was the ground): menus, selects, context and nested menus, the sheet and the bottom sheet are raised in their hairline frame, and their selected and hot rows (selected_on) are the hairline grey with an ink label. A popover keeps the ground. A nested menu's chevron (a row with a submenu) is the text grey: dim reads 2.93:1 on the raised panel in the light scheme.

Recomposing text keeps the cheap redraw

  • A stage that steps (Ui::on_step) or a subtree whose messages are its own is still drawn alone, the rest of the page copied, while a label or a slot's text recomposes glyph by glyph; before, the whole page was painted until the tiles landed. perf().counters.restages now counts only the frames that were drawn so.

Parts of a stage that change alone

  • El::steps() inside a stage marks a part of it that changes alone (a lens over a page that stays): a step (Ui::on_step) that names the stage views and draws only those parts, by their keys through Ui::stage_view, and the rest of the stage is the last frame's. A part stage_view does not give, or laid out otherwise, and the stage is viewed whole as before. The page naming the step no longer needs to name what in the stage moves.

Bloom in a region

  • El::bloom() marks what the look's bloom glows over: once any element is marked, the bloom glows only inside the box round what marked elements draw, and only from what is drawn there (Post::bloom_region). With none marked it covers the whole frame as before.

The default theme's colours

  • The default theme (DARK, LIGHT) takes the AA.9 table: new grounds, ink and accents (the accent is #f47a3c dark and #b23f0d light; #ff6b2b is retired), and four greys of its own (Theme::greys: raised, hairline, dim, text). Paint::DIM, TEXT and FAINT (now HAIRLINE, and RAISED new) are drawn as those colours exactly, not as the ink mixed toward the ground; a tone between two greys is drawn between their colours. Their tones moved to whole 255ths (dim 0.66 → 168/255, text 0.86 → 219/255). Other themes take their greys mixed as before (Greys::mixed); **a theme built from DARK or LIGHT with its own ground and ink keeps their greys unless it sets greys: Greys::mixed(ground, ink)** (or Greys::mixed_dim(ground, ink, 4.5), which takes dim toward the ink until it reads 4.5:1 on the ground).
  • Paint::EXTREME: white on a dark scheme, black on a light one (Theme::extreme). It is the ink image's fourth hue code (0.45, which the unused glyph-height code held).
  • El::fill_hover and El::fill_held: a fill under the pointer and while held or dragged; El::border_hover: a border under the pointer or with focus.
  • Variant::Primary: an ink plate with a ground label, the extreme under the pointer, for the one main action of a group. Variant::Accent stays for an explicitly coloured action. Badges take Primary too.
  • The default controls rest in ink: a switch that is on (ink track, ground knob; off a hairline track and a dim knob), a slider (ink run and thumb, a hairline rest; the thumb is the accent while dragged), a radio's dot, a checkbox's outline and tick, a toggle that is on and a choice's current option (ink plates with ground labels). So are a pagination's current page (an ink plate), a current tab's rule, a progress bar's fill and the label of a selected list row or menu option; widgets::SELECTED, the plate of a selected or keyed row, is the raised grey (it was the accent at 0.12), the hairline grey on a raised surface (widgets::selected_on). Hot rows of the command, context and nested menus are that plate with an ink label, a skip link an ink plate. Outlines of interactive controls stay dim at rest and turn ink under the pointer or with focus, and while a select or menu is open or a number is typed into (they were the accent then); panel frames (menus, dialogs, sheets, popovers, toasts, tooltips) are hairline. A switch's knob is the accent while pressed. The hover and focus marks are ink (Fx::marks_accent is 0 by default).
  • The recipes' and samples' main actions are Primary; the components' button page shows it.

Display type in its own material

  • near has ink bands (墨带, 1 to 3, and 带宽, px; 0 by default, the bands as before): only that many bands outside the ink, in the ink at 0.5, 0.3 and 0.15 (two: 0.5 and 0.25), each the width with as much ground before it, square round the ink's pixel corners, for pixel type on whole pixels and on the lattice.
  • carvr never cuts deeper than its band (刻带), the knife's wander included.
  • A glyph 32 px or more each way set in near or carvr takes all the ground its quad covers, padded by the spread once more, so what the material draws round the letter is drawn; text at reading sizes keeps the override just round its ink.

Cached subtrees

  • A cached subtree (El::cached) in which nothing it draws from changed is passed by when the page is painted: it is neither drawn nor hashed, and its plate is laid on as it was (Counters::kept counts them). It was drawn and hashed instance by instance on every paint (about 9 ms for 11,000 glyphs natively).

Shaders in Chrome

  • fs_ink's code test no longer mixes || and && without parentheses, which Chrome's WGSL compiler rejects (the ink, nearness and clearing passes were invalid there). A test now holds every WGSL source the renderer compiles to the expression rules Chrome enforces and naga does not.

Fast scrolls under dottr and charr

  • The frame in which a fast scroll's trace starts or stops holding the rows to the lattice moves the last frame by what it shows, instead of shading the whole viewport (the readout's latch on/off).
  • A lattice material used only outside the scrolled viewport (a bar in dottr over a list in charr) no longer stops the last frame from moving (the readout's latch: mixed lattices).

3D elements that come and go

  • A 3D element that comes on the page or goes (scrolled into view, a section opened) draws the frame again only where it is or was, not the frame whole (the readout's meshes); the 3D elements after it are not drawn again.

Pictures that arrive

  • A picture given after its page is drawn (Renderer::set_picture) is drawn on the next frame where it is, with the cells that read it, not by a frame drawn whole: a page need not turn Renderer::partial off for it.

Flows

  • What a new page lays out only after a flow began (a label shown once what it follows is in view, a drawing's new marks) comes in as what was new when the flow began: over a growing picture once it has landed. It was drawn at once where it is, outside the part of the picture that shows.
  • An element keyed the same on both pages that rode on the growing picture on the old page and is outside every flow on the new one (a layer over the page's frame: a pointer's box and its label) is carried by the picture to its new place. It was drawn at once there, outside the part of the picture that shows.

Stage scale

  • The stages' scale steps up only when the frames' cost there, by the level up's pixels, would be under 85 % of the display frame; a step down within 5 s of a step up holds the lower level, and a route's visit has at most two steps up (perf::STAGE_UPS, StageScale::held), in place of the waits of 5, 10 and 20 s and three returns a level.

Morphs from a stage

  • A morph from what a stage draws (El::stage; posed glyphs stepped every frame) starts its tiles where the frame before it drew them, also when the morph is asked for by a command with a new view that poses them elsewhere or hides them; it took them as the new view put them.

Marks in the ink

  • Fx::marks_accent (1 by default, the accent as before): how much of the accent the hover and focus marks are drawn in; 0 draws them in the ink.

Frame rate

  • Controls inside a subtree marked legible(false) keep their fill and label as at rest; a tab list's heads and rule are the control.
  • El::pass lets the pointer through to what is under it; El::opacity fades a subtree as one (at 0 it takes no pointer); El::commit_scope keeps a commit inside one element's box.
  • Ui::morph_from_last and MorphSpec::from_last fly an element's glyphs, each whole, from where they were drawn last frame to the new element's, across a route change, in every material.
  • The readout goes in Look::overlay_at's corner and moves on clockwise rather than cover text to read.
  • The INK leader is dashed and routed round text to read; Gaze::echo keeps fading earlier INK boxes.
  • With reduced motion, what comes and goes fades over 150 ms.
  • Looks: the thread's clearances move its control points, so the curve stays smooth; a thin accent rule under charr is one unbroken row; the Crack's pieces start to leave at 0.28; a press's flash in plain keeps a filled control's label at 4.5:1.

Materials named for what they do, and as source

  • Breaking: the six printing materials are named for what they do: dot → dottr, halftone → halftonr, ascii → charr, print → smudgr, lino → carvr, glitch → glitchr (the Material variants and their strings); plain, near and scribblr keep theirs. PicFx is Markr (El::markr), the finishing pass's Dither is Dithr (post.dithr, DithrKind), Cmd::Front / Cmd::FrontAt are Cmd::Ripplr / Cmd::RipplrAt, and El::ripple, El::ripple_scope and El::wipe are El::ripplr, El::ripplr_scope and El::wipr. No aliases: an old material name is refused and the error names the new one, and LookError gives the closest name for any other. Look recipes start with look:v2:; a look:v1: recipe is refused with the renames listed. Material has a variant for added materials, so cast with Material::id(), not as.
  • Materials as source (§1.6.4): each material is src/materials/<name>.wgsl with its name and numbers in a header; amenui add material <name> copies one into a project, and Look::material_source(name, wgsl, params) adds one back under a name of its own (ids 9–15, Material::Custom). Look::numbers(m) reads the numbers a material is drawn with.
  • El::effect(Effect { material, response, scope }) puts a material, a response and a scope on a subtree in one line, and el::wrap_text turns plain text or Markdown into a page to read.
  • The thread (§1.30): one large lopsided knot and smaller ones (knot_fall), a few chords escaping (escape), long flat tails with a few gentle bends (tail_turns) at half the line's tone (tail_tone), all in strand::ThreadShape; it no longer lingers between a half-drawn title and a thread when it draws back (strand::shown), and it passes behind pictures without changing colour.
  • Sample sheets that check themselves (amenui::sample, sheet --material <name> --loose … --arrival).
  • The audit finds text a material wears away at rest (Eroded, P1 under 85 % of plain's ink); a clipped finding names the side, the distance and what cuts it. Annotation tags and INK readouts keep off text to read and off each other.
  • near keeps a label in the ground's colour on a solid button whole. Text on a form fades its rim from edge 0.6 (range 0–0.8), and in form3d the pointer's light falls off smoothly.
  • The warm-up (§1.23): the material arrives on the page at rest, then over 120 ms (ui::RAMP) the page loosens to its caps and condenses, so the arrival no longer jumps.
  • Every public item of strand, fieldmap, field, hit, audit, form and materials has a doc comment.

Text on a form, the warm-up and the audit

  • Glyph poses have a depth and a facing (Pose::z, Pose::facing; the defaults draw as before), and a text element's glyphs are drawn far to near. El::form(FormFx { back, edge, overlap, fog }) keeps text on a form legible: what faces away is left out, what is edge on fades, a glyph mostly covered by nearer ones is not drawn (measured on the true ink), the far side is dimmer (§1.26).
  • amenui::form: Form wraps a block of text onto a sphere, a surface function or a mesh seen by a mesh::Camera (Form::pose_of), and Form::hit finds the pointer on the surface. The recipe form3d.
  • El::reveal_by(Reveal::Word | Line): clips, poses and the window show words and lines whole.
  • Hits follow what is drawn: Ui::glyph_at, Point::glyph and Point::z; El::turn turns an element and its subtree, and it is pressed where it is drawn; MeshHit has point, normal, t and uv, and Mesh::ray casts a ray at a mesh.
  • A button or link inside an element that drags (a scene with on_point) takes its own press.
  • The warm-up (§1.23): Renderer::begin draws plain at once, Warmup::step / Renderer::warm_step make the rest a piece at a time, and the page condenses into its look when it is ready; Offscreen::try_warming. Ui::prepare, Ui::ready and Ui::readiness run an application's own work in the frames' spare time; the annotation layer reads READY.
  • The audit (§1.31): amenui::audit, drive::audit, render --audit (with --strict, a P0 or P1 fails): text cut off, contrast, glyphs over each other, text too small, a page that never settles. The annotation layer's INK kind draws the same measured ink box.
  • What the audit found: Paint::DIM is 0.66 (it was 0.5, 3.2:1 on light); the light theme's accent is darker (5.6:1) and the selection tint 12 %; near keeps text's ink whole; the loading recipe no longer asks for a frame every tick.

Running applications

  • amenui::app: the framework runs applications itself. app::native::run opens a winit window (feature window, on by default) with the system clipboard, the input method, touch, frames on demand, and --shot / --exit-after for checks under Xvfb. app::web::Host and the web_app! macro generate a wasm module's exports from an application's state and message types. The App trait is how a shell routes, reads the route, takes clipboard requests, and sets the look, the language and reduced motion; every method has a default.
  • Native screen readers: app::access builds an AccessKit tree from the accessibility mirror for the window (UI Automation, NSAccessibility, AT-SPI) and turns Focus, Click, Increment and Decrement into the runtime's focus, activation and arrow keys.
  • The mirror says more: El::expanded, El::description, El::live (Role::Status is polite by default), El::invalid, El::required. The accordion, select and popover report expanded instead of checked.
  • Fonts beyond the bundled ones: Face::fallback for faces tried after the bundled ones, and Face::system() for the system's installed fonts (feature system-fonts, on by default; the native window adds it). Text::missing_glyphs. The complex-scripts feature for Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese line breaking.
  • Any language: Locale::Other(Tag) for any BCP 47 tag, falling back to English, with CLDR plural rules, right-to-left direction by language or script, and its own locale.name and locale.date. Arabic ships (Locale::AR, assets/i18n/ar.txt). Locale::ALL lists the three shipped locales; i18n::installed() lists the application's. Locale::name returns a String.
  • Focus: El::tab_order (positive first, 0 in document order, negative out of Tab), El::skip_to and El::focus_shown, and the skip_link component.
  • App::set_appearance (Appearance { dark, high_contrast }), App::files_hovering and App::files_dropped (Dropped with a path natively, name and bytes on the web), from both shells.
  • The native window draws with the bundled faces compiled in (text::embedded), so a shipped binary finds its fonts anywhere. WindowError::Font is gone.
  • amenui add writes from the sources compiled into it (sources::file, add::write replaces add::copy), so an installed command works without the repository.
  • Crate metadata for crates.io (description, keywords, categories, rust-version); the package leaves out CI (8.7 MiB compressed, under the 10 MiB limit).
  • The native window recovers from a lost GPU device: new device and renderer, setup again, the Ui untouched. native::run's setup is now Fn.
  • web/amenui.js, the amenui npm package: the page side of the web entry (mount, stage, css).
  • offscreen::read_back: one frame drawn into a texture of a given format and read back.
  • error::WindowError.
  • CI: clippy for native and web, rustdoc, the tests on lavapipe, and the website's tests against each framework commit.

The field map and trails, the thread material, small type, Loose Thread

  • Body is English only, whatever the interface language. Its language marking reads LANGUAGE EN / ZH, and pressing it changes the language of the rest of the gallery.
  • The field map (src/fieldmap.rs): a coarse grid over the window whose nodes hold energy and a flow direction. It steps at a fixed 1/60 s, spreads, decays by lifetime, meanders (meander) and creeps along ink (creep), returns to exactly zero within a bounded time, and needs no frames at rest.
  • Trails: an element with El::stirr stirs the map along the path it moved. Cmd::Stir stirs a point, an element or a stretch of a path. The floating box, the bottom sheet and the card stack trail by default; the preset sets how strongly (Normcore has none).
  • Reading the map: in the G-buffer stage it is one more source of looseness (still bounded by the legibility cap and each element's response), and instances that can be pushed drift along its flow (pictures and 3D elements loosen in place). The dot material's ground dots and glitch's ground read it; Ui::looseness uses the same value. An element that trails and has a ground fill is drawn on a layer above, so loose decoration it passes over does not show through it.
  • The #stirr page, with twelve numbers as number controls; one more line in the F2 readout; check item 19 (the former item 19, effects, is now item 17), bench … map and craft <dir> stirr.
  • The scribblr thread material (src/strand.rs). At rest it is byte-identical to plain. Loosened, the fill fades and the ink becomes one continuous thin line: it first follows the glyphs' true outlines, bridged into one stroke (clean outlined letters); looser, it drops vertices step by step and merges into long polylines across several glyphs that wander off. The one to three places with the most energy knot into large stars, one long tail enters from the scope's edge and another leaves through the opposite edge, and it writes itself back in reverse. Text to read and controls never turn into thread, fade or move. Eight numbers are in the material parameters (reach 40, knot_r 48, tail 480, angular 0.9), and Look::hold_ms holds the thread's pose for a beat.
  • Long paths with El::trace: a polyline drawn by progress, solid or dashed (dash, gap, by arc length from the start), bent by the flow, stirring the map at its head. "Draw a line" on #stirr.
  • The thread starts from the outline as the G-buffer stage drew it, plus the offset by which the map pushed the instance. Vertices are ranked by one Ramer–Douglas–Peucker pass, slide onto their chord and are then removed, so the line follows its chords and nothing jumps on the way back. Knots are long chords through a center. Tails are cut at the scope's edge and stop before text to read. strand::figure draws a tangle from a fixed seed as page content.
  • The #material-scribblr page; check item 20, bench … strand, craft <dir> scribblr and tests/strand.rs.
  • Letter spacing with El::tracking. Quiet type with El::whisper(): lowercase, 0.6 em tracking, in a serif (Source Serif 4 at its 8 pt optical size, Latin only, 82 KB, OFL).
  • Three new readouts in the annotation layer: edge notes EDGE (a hairline, ticks and a pointer mark at the scope's right edge, with the scope size, the pointer position, the material and theme, the loosest element and the number of non-zero map nodes set vertically), a waveform OSC (the energy of one row of nodes as a one-device-pixel polyline) and a level bar LEVEL (the mean energy). The annotation layer honours instance transforms, used only to turn the edge notes by 90°. The exported tree gains field: { level, nonzero }.
  • The tenth preset, Loose Thread: white thread on black (or paper white), one accent, scribblr, only edge notes and corner marks on hover; the map meanders, creeps along ink and lives two seconds (a title the pointer sweeps loosens past 0.8 and comes back within three seconds); the thread holds every other beat; a 700 ms transition loosens into thread and then draws the outline. Its style page is English only: a lowercase serif title, one picture, one sentence, three controls as one line of small type in the footer, and a drawn tangle at rest.
  • The finishing pass gains black and white only (binary: a black-and-white palette, no dither, line widths rounded; text, thread and annotations are thresholded by coverage rather than brightness, and pictures get a two-level ordered dither) and invert (invert: 1 − colour at the end).
  • Annotation corner marks sit 6 px outside the element's rect, off its own text.
  • Fixed theme ids and style-page descriptions that had drifted after themes and styles were removed.
  • check items 7.2 and 7.3 (edge notes, waveform, level bar, edge notes at 1080p) and 20.6 and 20.7 (black and white only, invert), three tests in tests/gaze.rs, the Loose Thread page in tests/i18n.rs, craft <dir> loose-thread and craft <dir> thread-ref.
