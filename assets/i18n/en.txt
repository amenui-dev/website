body.p1 = Before the tide turns, the flat is a sheet of grey water no deeper than a hand. Gulls stand in it as if on glass. The light comes in low from the east and lies along every ripple, so the surface is drawn in fine bright lines that shift whenever the wind changes its mind. Far out, a row of wooden posts marks a channel that only boats with shallow keels can use, and each post carries a small crown of weed that lifts and falls with the swell.
body.p2 = As the water drains away, the ground underneath shows its own pattern. Sand ridges run side by side for a while, part around a stone, and join again beyond it. Between them the hollows keep a little water, each a mirror the size of a plate. Worms leave coiled casts, crabs leave tracks like a row of stitches, and a heron walks slowly from one pool to the next, lifting each foot clear before it sets it down.
body.p3 = By noon the colour has gone out of everything. Sky, mud and sea are the same pale tone, and distance is hard to judge: a figure walking along the far edge could be near or very far. Sound carries instead. A spade striking a shell, a dog on the dyke, the faint tick of the mud as it dries in the sun all arrive clearly from places that cannot be seen.
body.p4 = In the afternoon the water comes back without a wave. It appears first in the channels, filling them from below, then spills over their edges in thin sheets that meet and widen. The ridges go under one by one. People who walked out turn back early, because the flat fills faster than it seems to, and the way home is a line of posts that grows shorter as the water rises around them.
body.p5 = At dusk only the posts remain, each with its reflection standing beneath it, and the gulls have moved to the dyke. The surface is still enough to hold the first lights of the village. Then the wind returns, the reflections break into short strokes, and the whole flat is drawn again in the fine lines it began the day with.
